# Wild Ferns — Brand Voice Profile

*A reference for writing web copy, product descriptions, emails, and social posts that sound authentically like Wild Ferns.*

Sources: [wildferns.co.nz](https://wildferns.co.nz/) (Story, Who We Are, Shop and product pages) plus internal Wild Ferns / Parrs email correspondence.

---

## 1. Who is speaking

Wild Ferns® is the skincare brand of **Parrs Products Ltd** — a 100% New Zealand-owned family company established in Auckland in 1951 by Des and Margaret Parr. Three generations of the Parr family — descendants of floriculturists — now run it. In the 1980s Greg and Janet Parr were the first to put Rotorua's Therapeutic Thermal Mud and Manuka Honey into skincare.

The voice should always feel like it comes from **a proud, warm, family-run New Zealand maker** — not a faceless corporate cosmetics brand. FernMark-certified, genuinely local, a little bit humble about its long history.

**Core belief that anchors everything:** *"If you take care of nature, nature will take care of you."*

---

## 2. Voice in one line

> **Warm, natural, and quietly proud** — a caring New Zealand family sharing the treasures of their homeland to nourish your skin.

---

## 3. Voice attributes (the "we are / we are not")

| We ARE | We are NOT |
|---|---|
| Warm and caring | Clinical or cold |
| Natural and earthy | Synthetic or "lab-tech" |
| Proud of New Zealand | Boastful or arrogant |
| Nurturing and sensory | Aggressive or salesy |
| Trustworthy, heritage-backed | Trend-chasing or hype-driven |
| Gently premium | Elitist or exclusive |
| Genuine and personal | Generic or templated |

---

## 4. Tone by context

- **Product pages:** Sensory and benefit-led. Open with the feeling or result, then the hero ingredient, then the reassurance (natural, made in NZ, no nasties).
- **Brand / About pages:** Storytelling and heartfelt. Lean into family, generations, place, and connection to nature.
- **Email & newsletters:** Friendly and personal, like a note from someone who knows you. Warm greeting, one clear message, gentle invitation — never pushy.
- **Social:** Bright, approachable, a touch playful (🐝🌸). Celebrate ingredients, NZ landscapes, and community.
- **Customer service / reviews:** Gracious, humble, solution-focused. Thank first, help second.

---

## 5. Signature vocabulary

**Words the brand reaches for:**
nourish · nourishing · nurture · revitalise · rejuvenate · replenish · radiant · radiance · glow · vitality · brightness · goodness · treasures · pristine · pure · natural · gentle · luxurious · crafted · celebrate · complexion · moisture · hydration

**Ingredient hero-words (name them proudly):**
Manuka Honey 80+ · Bee Venom · Thermal Mud (Rotorua) · Manuka Oil · Kiwifruit · Shea Butter · botanical extracts

**Place & heritage words:**
New Zealand · Aotearoa · the place we call home · pristine landscapes · flora and fauna · family · three generations · pioneers · made with special care

**Reassurance phrases:**
no parabens · no mineral oil · no sodium lauryl sulphate · not tested on animals · environmentally-friendly packaging · made in New Zealand

**Note on spelling:** Use **New Zealand / British English** — *revitalise, moisturise, crème, colour, nourishing*. Not American spellings. Manuka is often styled **Mānuka** (with the macron) on newer copy — keep it consistent within a piece.

---

## 6. Sentence-level style

- **Speak to "your skin" and "you."** Make the reader the subject of the benefit.
- **Lead with the sensory payoff, follow with the reason.** E.g. *"Wake up to a complexion that is rejuvenated and vibrant, basking in the radiance of skin that has been lavished with the goodness of Manuka Honey."*
- **Layer three benefits in a rhythm** — brands like this favour triplets: *"all-day moisture, brightness, and vitality."*
- **Use gentle, generous verbs** — *lavished, basking, crafted, harvested, celebrates.*
- **Keep sentences flowing and a little lyrical** on brand pages; **tighter and clearer** on product and email.
- **Anchor claims in nature and place**, not in laboratory language, even when the product is scientifically formulated.

**Social caption:**
> "Straight from Rotorua's geothermal heart 🌿 our Thermal Mud has been drawing out the day since the 1980s. Nature's original glow-up. 🐝"

## 8. Quick do / don't

**Do**
- Name the hero ingredient and where it comes from.
- Reassure with "no nasties" and "made in New Zealand."
- Sound like a caring family, not a corporation.
- Celebrate nature and place with genuine pride.

**Don't**
- Over-promise "miracle" or "instant" results.
- Use clinical/synthetic language as the lead.
- Be pushy, gimmicky, or hype-driven.
- Drop the New Zealand identity — it's the brand's whole heart.

---

## 9. One-paragraph summary (paste into a brief or an AI prompt)

> Write as **Wild Ferns**, a warm, family-run New Zealand natural-skincare brand (est. 1951, three generations of the Parr family). The voice is nurturing, sensory, and quietly proud of Aotearoa. Speak directly to "you" and "your skin," lead with the feeling or result, then name the natural hero ingredient (Manuka Honey 80+, Bee Venom, Rotorua Thermal Mud, Kiwifruit, Manuka Oil) and where it comes from, and reassure with "made in New Zealand, no parabens or nasties, never tested on animals." Use New Zealand/British spelling (nourish, revitalise, crème, radiance). Favour generous, lyrical verbs and benefit triplets. Never be clinical, pushy, or hype-driven — sound like a caring family sharing the treasures of their homeland.
