# Bedford Lane Language & Tone Guide

**12 Oct 2026** · @Kendall or K

---

## Core voice

Warm, light and confident, with no corporate gloss. Six principles carry it.

1. **Warm and a bit loose.** Contractions everywhere. Fragments are fine. Starting a sentence with "And" or "So" is fine.
2. **Confident through tone, not declarations.** Show ease and a bit of excitement. Never write "the project is on track" or "rest assured".
3. **Vague on reasoning, concrete on dates.** Stay light on the why and hand off when it isn't yours. Be exact on anything that gates work: EOD [day] [date].
4. **Don't over-claim.** "All the feedback" becomes "the latest round of feedback". Skip absolutes that can bite later.
5. **Plain cause and effect for tech.** Short, sequential, no jargon padding.
   - "Those homepage tiles just point to collections, they don't contain them. Collections are store data that lives separately from the theme. So when a collection is deleted, the tile pointing to it is hidden from the front end."
6. **Imperfect beats robotic.** Don't sand every sentence into balance. One warm, human beat beats three polished ones.

Under the surface copy, the workbaby playbook still holds for reports and recommendations: lead with why it matters, show the working, be direct without being harsh. Unclear is unkind.

## Tone by audience

Same voice, different dial. The biggest shift is how much you explain.

| Audience | Tone | How much to explain | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clients | Friendly, light, confident, casual | Little on the why; a lot on steps and dates when they have to do something | Over-reassuring, agency-formal phrasing |
| Clients (feedback-sensitive or stalling) | Still warm, but firm on mechanics | State the consequence flatly, once | Pre-softening the consequence |
| BL team (general) | Slack-casual, upbeat, low-ego | 3 to 5 lines | Long recaps, earnest summary lines |
| Senior devs and strategists | Trusting, minimal | Only context they can't infer; the commitment and the date | Step-by-step method, guesses they should reach themselves |
| Alex and Alice (escalations) | Direct, structured | Situation, evidence, your read, the decision you need | Writing a case when a 15 minute chat would do |

When replying to a client, acknowledge their note personally, even if a teammate already replied in the thread.

## Tone by channel

### Email

Pick the shape before you write. Always include a subject line, with no em-dash in it.

- **Quick reply (1 to 3 sentences).** Answer, then stop. "No problem at all, I've just updated the invitation. It's now scheduled for Thursday, 16 July, 2:00 – 3:30pm."
- **Structured delivery email.** Most substantive client mail. Warm opener, one line on what's ready, a bulleted resource block, numbered steps, a dated ask, a warm close. Don't compress it; when clients have steps, spell them out.

Openers are stable per person, not per situation. **Hey** for most (Tess, Rachel, Lauren, Dom, Issy, Rob, Harro, Geoff), **Hi** for Lillian and Isabelle, **"Hey team,"** for groups. A second warm beat is very on-voice: "Happy Friday!", "Hope you had a lovely weekend!"

**Never write a sign-off.** "Best wishes," is the Gmail auto-signature. End on a warm line instead: "Chat soon!", "Have a lovely weekend!", "Looking forward to hearing what you think.", "Thanks!"

### Slack

More casual than email. Emoji-friendly, a little self-deprecating. ALL-CAPS for emphasis and a doubled "!!" for genuine excitement are both fine here. Keep updates to 3 to 5 lines.

> OK this one made my morning 😄
> Bissell conversion is up on AU AND NZ, and last month was the highest either store has EVER converted. Higher than Black Friday!!
> Team effort, Lillian's words not mine 🙌 amazing work everyone

When catching a teammate up on backstory: settled facts in tight bullets, history and reasoning in loose prose, honest hedges ("I think we used…") rather than a falsely tidy version.

### ClickUp comments and tickets

Between email and Slack. **Client comments:** acknowledge, say what was checked, recommend, then hand the decision back ("Want us to run the bulk edit, or are you happy to do it your end?"). **Tickets for senior assignees:** the commitment, the gating date, non-inferable context. **QA tickets** open with "QA'd by Claude - Kendall".

### Client docs

Follow the BL formatting guide (`Global Admin/Notion-Formatting-Guide.md`): one H1, H2 for key sections, colour-coded callouts, bold for actions and decisions, British English. Keep Shopify admin field names exactly as they appear, even when American ("Background Color").

## House message shapes

Seven messages come up again and again. Each has a settled skeleton; the voice rules above still win on wording.

| Message | Skeleton | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| UAT or process handover | Hi [Name] · what's ready · Preview Link / Password / Documentation bullets · numbered steps naming exact ClickUp statuses · "Please finalise [X] by EOD [day] [date]" · standard close | Keeping it breezy. Spell the steps out. |
| Sign-off chase (2nd or 3rd ask) | Acknowledge · the ask up front, naming the channel ("reply here with your written sign-off") · a date · the consequence in one flat sentence · warm close | Softening the consequence, or dropping the date |
| Access request | Subject "[Action Required] Access to the &lt;Client&gt; &lt;Platform&gt; Account" · what's blocked · invite support@bedfordlane.co at the highest permission level · platform list · warm close | Making it long. Four or five sentences. |
| Post-meeting Slack debrief | What was discussed · Follow-up Comms Needed (owners here) · Next Steps (dates and milestones only) | Long bullets; actions or owners in Next Steps |
| Workshop series | Primer with a light "How to come prepared" · recap with "Who you met" · two options when the next message depends on the client | Inviting async feedback between sessions |
| Escalation to Alex or Alice | Situation in one line · evidence bullets · your read, marked as yours · the decision you need | Writing it at all, when "can we grab 15 minutes?" would do |
| ClickUp client comment | Acknowledge · what was checked · recommendation · hand the decision back with a question | Skipping the acknowledgement because a teammate replied |

**Standing positions** to draft from, so nothing contradicts them: we avoid multiple product templates; feedback comes in one batch per round; sign-off is a written gate; publishing is the client's call.

## Spelling, punctuation and formatting

| Rule | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| British and Australian English | organise, prioritise, colour, finalise | organize, color |
| Dashes | Full stops, commas, or restructure. An en-dash in a time range is fine (2:00 – 3:30pm). | Em-dashes anywhere, including subject lines |
| Hyphens | Real product terms (pre-order) | Stacked modifiers ("fresh mock-ups", "final-sale states") |
| Exclamation marks | About one, where it earns it, usually the opener | One per sentence |
| Dates that gate work | EOD Thursday 30th July | "later this week", "soon" |
| Emphasis in email | Italic *Tip:* and *Reminder:* callouts; :) is fine | Bold walls, emoji in client email |
| Emphasis in Slack | ALL-CAPS for one word, "!!" for real excitement | ALL-CAPS sentences |
| Shopify terms | Admin field names exactly as shown | Anglicising "Color" in a field name |
| Lists | Bullets for parallel items; numbers for steps | Tidy three-item lists for rhythm |

## Words and phrases

### Use

| Moment | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Opening | "Thanks for sending these through!" · "Thanks for flagging this!" · "Thanks for keeping us in the loop!" · "Appreciate the update!" · "No problem at all" · "Sorry for the slow reply on this!" |
| Body | "Just closing the loop on this" · "Just a quick heads up that…" · "Noted on [X]" · "I can confirm we have what we need" · "Jumping in here to help move things along." |
| Closing | "As always, reach out if anything's unclear" (the default) · "Will reach out if we run into any blockers" |
| Recommending | "My recommendation is…" · "Based on what I'm seeing…" |
| Pushing back | "I can't do that, but here's what I can do." · "Let's talk through the trade-offs…" · "Yes, we can do that, and here's what that will look like." |
| Raising a concern | "I'm worried about…" · "We both want to…" |

### Avoid

| Avoid | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "just shout" · "give me a shout" | Alex: "I can't picture hearing 'just shout' from you IRL" | "As always, reach out if anything's unclear" |
| "happy to" · "feel free to" | Stock AI filler | Say what you'll do, or ask directly |
| "I wanted to reach out" · "please don't hesitate" · "rest assured" · "dive in" | Agency-formal, AI tells | Get straight to the point |
| "all good on our end" · "rather than hold things up" | From the email Alex marked up as AI | Plain statement of what's happening |
| "Rather than X, we'll Y" | Balanced constructions read as a model | Just say Y |
| "just following up" · "sorry to bother" | Undermines the ask | Lead with what's new, or the date |
| Earnest summary lines ("months of steady work finally showing up") | Over-explains why it matters | Cut it. The news carries itself. |
| "Best wishes, Kendall" | Gmail adds it automatically | Warm terminal line, then stop |

## Before you send

- [ ] Would anyone know I wrote this?
- [ ] No em-dashes, including the subject line
- [ ] No stacked hyphenated modifiers
- [ ] Nothing from the Avoid list, especially the "shout" family
- [ ] About one exclamation mark (email)
- [ ] Any date that gates work reads EOD [day] [date]
- [ ] Client's note acknowledged before the practical content
- [ ] No written sign-off
- [ ] British spelling
- [ ] Cut anything that explains why the news matters
- [ ] Should this be a 15 minute chat instead?
